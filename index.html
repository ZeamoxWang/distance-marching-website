Distance Marching for Generative Modeling

No time input, yet competitive with time-conditioned flow matching.

TL;DR

We model a distance field of the data manifold and generate by descending it, without time input. Our losses emphasize closer denoising targets, making ours the only model without time input that stays competitive with time-conditioned models across datasets and architectures.

Method

No time input: a two-layer CNN head predicts the distance \(u_\theta(\mathbf{x})\) to the data manifold and feeds it to the unchanged backbone (U-Net or SiT) in place of the time embedding. The backbone predicts the denoising direction \(\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x})\). The head adds only 0.009%–0.19% parameters.

One-Step Loss

$$\frac{\big\lVert \mathbf{x} - u_\theta(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{s} \big\rVert^2}{\lVert \mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s}\rVert^2 + \epsilon}$$

Emphasizes closer data points, mitigating the bias toward the data mean.

Directional Eikonal Loss

$$\Big\lVert \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - \frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s}}{\sqrt{\lVert \mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s}\rVert^2 + c_0}} \Big\rVert^2$$

Disambiguates the offset \(C\) of the learned field as \(c_0\) by constraining the direction.

\(\mathbf{x}\) linearly interpolates Gaussian noise and a data sample \(\mathbf{s}\). From Gaussian noise, two deterministic samplers march toward the data manifold with step size \(\eta\):

Sphere Tracing

$$\mathbf{x} \leftarrow \mathbf{x} - \eta\, u_\theta(\mathbf{x})\, \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x})$$

Uses both the distance and the directional information.

Gradient Descent

$$\mathbf{x} \leftarrow \mathbf{x} - \eta\, \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x})$$

Uses only the directional information.
